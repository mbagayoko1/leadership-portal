# Source check: module guide concepts (English)

**Checked:** `src/guides/en.js`, all 12 guides (m1 to m11, vocal): every concept with a named origin (`src`), the definitions (`d`) of those concepts, unsourced concepts that make a definite factual claim, the `def` and `outcomes` fields, and the real-world statistics in the worked examples (`en.js` and `examples-en.js`).
**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Method:** web search plus reading pages. Wherever possible the source is a primary one: the publisher's page, the original paper or proceedings record, or the originating organisation's own material. Wikipedia was used only to find primary sources and is never cited. Where no primary page could be loaded, the table says so.

**Result:** 26 attributions checked. 22 are confirmed as written. 3 are correct but need a change (Mendelow, GROW, Fisher & Ury). 1 has an optional clarification (Schein, add the year). No `def` or `outcomes` field makes a checkable factual claim. The one real statistic is correct.

## Applied on 2026-11-20

Corrections 1 to 6 below were applied in `src/guides/en.js` and, where the
French text had the same issue, in `src/guides/fr.js`. The French m2 example
already used the right pronoun.

The same pass also checked the vocabulary (`vocab-en.js`, `vocab-fr.js`) and
the extra worked examples (`examples-en.js`), and made two corrections:

| Term | Before | After | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| diaphragm | "The large muscle below the lungs that controls breathing and supports the voice." | "The dome-shaped muscle below the lungs that contracts to draw air in." Watch out: "You cannot push sound out with the diaphragm; breath support means controlling the out-breath steadily." | The diaphragm is the main muscle of breathing *in*. It relaxes as you speak, so it cannot push the voice out. |
| resonance | "…as sound vibrates in the chest, mouth and face." | "…as sound is amplified in the throat, mouth and nose." | The voice resonates in the vocal tract (throat, mouth and nose). "Chest voice" is a sensation, not where resonance happens. |

The one factual claim in the extra examples ("girls who stay in school marry
later, earn more and raise healthier children") is supported by the World
Bank's *Missed Opportunities: The High Cost of Not Educating Girls* (Wodon et
al., 2018). The rest of the examples are fictional scenes by design.

## Recommended corrections

Apply the same changes to the matching entries in `src/guides/fr.js`: line 148 (Mendelow), 409 (Fisher & Ury), 448 (GROW) and optionally 362 (Schein).

1. **m4, "Stakeholder mapping", `src`**
   `"Aubrey Mendelow (1991)"` → `"Adapted from Aubrey Mendelow (1981)"`
   The paper appeared in the 1981 ICIS proceedings, and the 1981 model uses power and dynamism, not interest.

2. **m10, "Principled negotiation", `d`**
   `"Separating people from the problem, focusing on interests rather than positions, and looking for options that serve both sides."`
   → `"Separating the people from the problem, focusing on interests rather than positions, inventing options for mutual gain and insisting on objective criteria."`
   This restores the fourth element of the method.

3. **m11, "Coaching with the GROW model", `src`**
   `"John Whitmore, Coaching for Performance (1992)"`
   → `"Graham Alexander, Alan Fine and John Whitmore; first published in Whitmore, Coaching for Performance (1992)"`
   The model was developed by all three, and Whitmore was the first to publish it.

4. **m2, example 1, `changes[3]`** (pronoun)
   `"He ends with a plan, so the director does not need to ask a follow-up."` → `"She ends with a plan, so the director does not need to ask a follow-up."`

5. *(Optional)* **m9, "Three levels of culture", `src`**
   `"Edgar Schein, Organizational Culture and Leadership"` → `"Edgar Schein, Organizational Culture and Leadership (1985)"`
   This adds the first-edition year, consistent with the other entries.

6. *(Optional)* **m5, "One message", `why`**
   `"Audiences forget most of a talk within days. One clear idea survives."` → `"Audiences forget much of a talk within days. One clear idea survives."`
   No source supports "most" as a precise claim.

## Real-world statistic: "more than a billion young people aged 15 to 24"

**Where it appears:** m5, example 1 (`first` version of the speech).

**Verdict: confirmed.**

- UN DESA, *World Youth Report 2018*, executive summary: 1.2 billion young people aged 15–24, 16% of the world's population (World Population Prospects 2017). [Source](https://desapublications.un.org/sites/default/files/publications/2022-03/Executive-Summary_World-Youth-Report_07.12.18-1.pdf)
- UN DESA, *World Youth Report 2019*, key messages: about 1.2 billion aged 15–24, 16% of the world's population, expected to peak at nearly 1.4 billion around 2065 (WPP 2019). [Source](https://social.desa.un.org/sites/default/files/migrated/21/2019/08/WYP2019_10-Key-Messages_GZ_8AUG19.pdf). This PDF returned 403 when fetched on the check date, so its wording comes from the search index.
- *World Youth Report 2020* (as cited by UNESCO-UNEVOC): 1.21 billion, 15.5%.
- UN DESA released *World Population Highlights 2026: Youth* this year, but its page returned 403 when fetched, so its current count was not read. Every recent UN figure is about 1.2 to 1.3 billion, which "more than a billion" covers safely.
- **Watch out:** UNFPA's *State of World Population 2014* ("The Power of 1.8 Billion") counts people aged **10–24**, not 15–24. Do not swap that figure into this sentence. [Source](https://www.unfpa.org/swop-2014)

The other figures in the examples (15% overspend, 30% funding cut, 62% budget execution and so on) belong to fictional scenes and were not checked.
